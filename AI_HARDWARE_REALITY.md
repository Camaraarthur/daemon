# AI Hardware Reality Check

**Last updated: April 2026**

The brutal truth about every AI hardware/wearable product that launched in 2023-2025. Not the hype, not the funding rounds -- the actual sales numbers and what happened.

---

## The Dead

### 1. Humane AI Pin -- DEAD

- **Units sold:** ~10,000 (target was 100,000)
- **Price:** $699 + $24/month subscription
- **Total raised:** $241 million
- **Burned:** $230 million
- **Sold to:** HP for $116 million (Feb 2025) -- an 86% markdown from peak $1B valuation
- **Returns exceeded sales** by summer 2024. Over $1M in product returned.
- **Shut down:** Feb 28, 2025 at 12pm PT. All cloud services killed. Devices bricked remotely.
- **Refund policy:** Only last 90 days of buyers. Everyone else got a paperweight.
- **What HP actually bought:** 300+ patents and the "Cosmos" AI platform. Not the hardware business.
- **Why it failed:** $700 for a device that was "bad at almost everything it does." Slow, unreliable, confusing UX. Ex-Apple designers who forgot that Apple ships things that work. No screen, no speaker that worked well, projected display was a gimmick. The product was a demo reel, not a product.

### 2. Friend (formerly Tab) -- EFFECTIVELY DEAD

- **Units sold:** ~3,000 ordered, ~1,000 actually shipped
- **Revenue:** $348,000 (as of Sept 2025)
- **Total raised:** ~$8 million
- **Spent on domain friend.com:** $1.8 million
- **Spent on NYC/LA ad campaign:** millions (11,000 ads in NYC subway alone, $1M+ campaign that got defaced with graffiti)
- **Spent on YouTube commercial:** $250,000
- **Revenue from hardware:** ~$150,000 at $130/unit on the units actually shipped
- **Current status:** Pivoted to web chatbot (Oct 2025). The pendant is effectively abandoned.
- **Why it failed:** 22-year-old founder (Avi Schiffmann) spent almost all the money on marketing and a domain name, not on making a product people wanted. The pendant was a microphone + Bluetooth chip running Gemini. It texts you commentary on your life. Nobody wanted that. The NYC ad campaign became a meme -- people graffitied every ad. Schiffmann called it "capitalism as art." Investors called it burning cash.

### 3. Open Interpreter 01 Light -- DEAD (never shipped)

- **Units sold:** 0
- **Pre-order price:** $109
- **Status:** Refunded all pre-orders (Sept 2024). Never manufactured.
- **Pivoted to:** Free iOS/Android app (01 App)
- **Why it failed:** The team realized "it should have been an app." They were right. An ESP32 with a mic doing voice commands to your laptop didn't need to be a physical product.

## The Acqui-Hired (Team Bought, Product Killed)

### 4. Limitless (formerly Rewind) -- ACQUIRED BY META (Dec 2025)

- **Revenue:** $2.2M ARR (April 2024). Split: $1.3M pendant sales + $100K Limitless app + $2.1M legacy Rewind app
- **Growth:** 207% YoY
- **Pendant price:** $99
- **Units sold:** Not disclosed publicly. Revenue suggests low thousands at best.
- **Total raised:** $33 million
- **Acquired by:** Meta (Dec 2025). Undisclosed price.
- **Status:** Stopped selling pendants Dec 5, 2025. Existing users supported for 1 year. Rewind app sunsetted.
- **Why it was acquired:** Meta wanted the team and the always-on recording tech for Ray-Ban Meta glasses, not the pendant business. The pendant was a proof of concept.

### 5. Bee AI -- ACQUIRED BY AMAZON (July 2025)

- **Product:** $49.99 bracelet + $19/month subscription
- **Units sold:** Not disclosed
- **Total raised:** $7 million
- **Acquired by:** Amazon for undisclosed sum
- **Status:** Still shipping under Amazon. Team of 8 building new features.
- **Why acquired:** Amazon wanted always-listening AI tech for Alexa/Echo ecosystem. The bracelet was a research vehicle. Four major updates shipped since acquisition (Actions, Daily Insights, voice notes).

## The Walking Dead

### 6. Rabbit R1 -- ALIVE BUT BARELY

- **Units sold:** 100,000-130,000 (impressive initial hype)
- **Price:** $199
- **Daily active users:** 5,000 (Sept 2024). That's <5% of units sold actually being used.
- **Revenue:** ~$20M from hardware sales (est. based on units x price)
- **Employee situation:** Employees not paid since July 2025. Some on strike since October 2025.
- **Status:** CEO Jesse Lyu claims new funding round signed (legally binding term sheet). Teasing next-gen "three-in-one" device for 2026.
- **16 software updates** shipped to fix the broken launch product
- **Why it's struggling:** The R1 was an Android app in a $200 orange box. The "Large Action Model" didn't work. Jesse Lyu's demo was largely faked. 95% of buyers stopped using it. The company went into "cash conservative mode" after India expansion failed. They literally couldn't pay their employees.

### 7. Omi (Based Hardware) -- EARLY STAGE, UNPROVEN

- **Product:** $89 pendant (consumer), $70 dev kit
- **Units sold:** Not disclosed. Likely very low (seed-stage company).
- **Total raised:** $2.7 million ($700K initial + $2M from Tim Draper, Jan 2025)
- **Status:** Shipping Q2 2025. Open-source software stack. HIPAA-compliant.
- **Differentiator:** Only open-source platform. Python scripting for conversation analysis.
- **Reality check:** $2.7M raised for a hardware company is not enough to scale manufacturing. This is a dev community play, not a consumer product yet.

## The One That Actually Worked

### Plaud AI (Note / NotePin) -- THE ONLY COMMERCIAL SUCCESS

- **Units sold:** 1,000,000+ globally
- **Revenue:** $100M annualized (Nov 2024), on track for $250M in 2025. Bootstrapped to $180M ARR.
- **Product:** $159 NotePin AI voice recorder
- **Employees:** Small team
- **External funding:** $0. Completely self-funded. Profitable.
- **Growth:** 10x annually for two consecutive years
- **Why it works:**
  - It solves a real, specific problem: recording and transcribing meetings
  - It's not trying to be your friend, replace your phone, or project a screen on your hand
  - It's a better voice recorder with AI transcription. That's it.
  - $159 is impulse-buy territory for professionals
  - 300 free transcription minutes/month creates ongoing value
  - No subscription required for basic functionality
  - It's a tool, not a lifestyle product
  - The company is Chinese (founder Xu Gao), operates lean, sells overseas-first
  - Zero marketing spend initially -- grew through product quality

## Why AI Hardware Fails

Every failed product shares the same pattern:

1. **Solution looking for a problem.** Humane, Friend, and Rabbit all started with "what if AI was a physical thing?" instead of "what specific problem needs solving?" Plaud started with "meetings need better transcription."

2. **Phones already exist.** The AI Pin tried to replace your phone. The R1 tried to be a phone-adjacent device. Friend tried to be a texting companion. All of them did things your phone already does, but worse. Plaud doesn't try to replace your phone -- it's a peripheral that does one thing your phone does poorly (long-form recording + transcription).

3. **Hardware is hard, and VC timelines don't match.** Building hardware takes 18-24 months. VCs want growth in 6-12 months. This creates pressure to ship half-baked products (R1) or spend all your money on marketing before the product is ready (Friend).

4. **The "wow" demo problem.** Humane's TED talk was incredible. Rabbit's CES demo was electric. Friend's ad campaign was viral. None of that matters if the daily experience is frustrating. Hardware lives or dies on the 100th use, not the first.

5. **Subscription dependency on hardware margins.** At $699 + $24/month, Humane needed both hardware sales AND retention. They got neither. Plaud charges $159 with free basic features -- the hardware sale IS the business.

6. **40% of AI startups launched in 2024 were dead by early 2026.** The sector-wide failure rate is brutal and accelerating.

## The Bottom Line for Daemon

The data is clear:

1. **No standalone AI hardware pendant/pin has commercially succeeded** except Plaud, and Plaud is a voice recorder with AI features, not an "AI companion" or "AI assistant device."

2. **The acqui-hires (Limitless -> Meta, Bee -> Amazon) tell you something:** Big tech wants the listening/recording tech for their existing platforms (Ray-Ban glasses, Alexa), not as standalone products.

3. **Every "AI companion" hardware product has failed.** Friend, Humane, and the companion angle of Rabbit all bombed. People don't want a new device that's their friend. They want tools that do specific things well.

4. **The only path for AI hardware is:**
   - Solve ONE specific problem better than a phone can
   - Be a tool, not a companion
   - Price it where the value is obvious ($99-$159, not $699)
   - Don't depend on subscriptions for survival
   - Be profitable from hardware margins or die

5. **Software wins.** ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Midjourney -- all of them deliver more value than any AI hardware product ever has. The interface is the phone/laptop you already own.

6. **If you're building AI hardware, the question isn't "how do we make AI physical?" It's "what can this form factor do that a phone literally cannot?"** For Plaud, the answer is "sit on a table recording an 8-hour meeting without draining your phone battery or looking weird." That's a real answer. "Be your friend" is not.
